Graphs · Part 2 of 3

Flip it, stretch it, squeeze it

A minus sign flips a graph. A number that multiplies stretches or squeezes it. Each one follows Part 1's rule: inside changes x, the opposite way. Outside changes y, the same way.

Do Part 1 first. Part 3 puts several moves together. No calculator needed.

3 · Outside

A number multiplying outside stretches it up and down

Vertical stretch or shrink y = a · f(x):   (x, y) → (x, a · y) outside, so y changes, the same way: times a
  • a > 1: taller. A vertical stretch.
  • 0 < a < 1: flatter. A vertical shrink.

4 · Inside

A number multiplying inside stretches it sideways, the opposite way

Horizontal stretch or shrink y = f(bx):   (x, y) → (xb, y) inside, so x changes, the opposite way: times b becomes divide by b
  • b > 1: narrower, squeezed toward the y-axis. A horizontal shrink.
  • 0 < b < 1: wider, pulled away from the y-axis. A horizontal stretch.
On √xOn √2x: x2On √½x: x½ = 2x

Why the examples use √x

On x2 and |x|, a sideways squeeze gives the same graph as a vertical stretch: (2x)2 = 4x2 and |2x| = 2|x|. Practise sideways moves on √x or on a graph made of straight pieces.
