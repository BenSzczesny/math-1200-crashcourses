Fractions with variables · Simplifying

Only factors cancel. Terms don't.

Almost every wrong cancellation is the same mistake: crossing off something that was added, not multiplied. This page shows how to tell the difference.

You'll need this for simplifying, multiplying and dividing fractions with variables. No calculator needed.

1 · What it is

Cancelling is dividing

When you cancel, you divide the top and the bottom by the same thing. That doesn't change the fraction's value.

Notice: 6 and 9 had to be written as products first. Only then is there a shared factor to divide out.

The only rule You may cancel a factor. You may not cancel a term.
2 · Two words

Factor or term?

Factors are multiplied
2 · x · (x + 3) Three factors. These can cancel.
Terms are added or subtracted
2 + x − 3x2 Three terms. These can't cancel.
How to tell

Look at what joins the piece to the rest. Joined by + or −: a term. Joined by multiplication: a factor.

A bracket counts as one object. So in 2(x + 3), the bracket is a factor, even though there's a + inside it.

Drill: is the highlighted piece a factor or a term?

3 · The most common mistake

Cancelling across a plus sign

Allowed: 3 is a factor on top
The top is 3 times (x + 1), so the 3s cancel.
Not allowed: 3 is a term on top
Here the 3 is added to x. There's nothing to divide out.

When the bottom is a single number, you can divide it into the top. But it has to reach every term:

Simplify6x + 93
  1. 6x3 + 93Divide each term on top by 3.
  2. 2x + 36x ÷ 3 = 2x and 9 ÷ 3 = 3.

4 · The fix

Factor first, then cancel

If the top or bottom is a sum, you're not stuck. Factor it. Factoring turns a sum into a product, and products have factors that can cancel.

Simplifyx2 − 4x + 2
  1. (x − 2)(x + 2)x + 2Factor the top (difference of squares). Same value, but now (x + 2) is visibly a factor.
  2. x − 2Cancel the (x + 2). (This holds for every x except −2, where the original isn't defined.)

Pulling out a common factor (the GCF) works the same way:

Simplify4x2 + 8x6x
  1. 4x(x + 2)6xPull out the common factor 4x on top.
  2. 2 · 2x(x + 2)3 · 2xNow top and bottom share the factor 2x.
  3. 2(x + 2)3Cancel 2x. The bracket stays whole.

5 · Signs

Minus signs and brackets

The minus reaches everything
−(x − 3) = −x + 3 It means (−1)(x − 3). Both terms get multiplied by −1, so both signs change.
A backwards subtraction
3 − x = −(x − 3) Flip a subtraction around and you get its negative.

That second fact rescues fractions that look stuck:

Simplify3 − xx − 3
  1. −(x − 3)x − 3Pull out −1 on top: 3 − x = −(x − 3).
  2. −1Now the brackets match, so they cancel. The −1 is left (for x ≠ 3).
