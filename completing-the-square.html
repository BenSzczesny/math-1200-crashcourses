Half it, square it, add it to both sides

1 · The goal

Two ways to write the same circle

General form
x2 + y2 − 8x − 4y + 16 = 0 Center? Radius? You can't tell by looking.
Standard form
(x − 4)2 + (y − 2)2 = 4 Center (4, 2), radius 2. You read them straight off.

Completing the square turns the left one into the right one.

2 · Warm-up

Squaring a bracket gives three terms, not two

Multiply out(x + 3)2
  1. (x + 3)(x + 3)Squared means times itself.
  2. x2 + 3x + 3x + 9Each term in the first bracket times each term in the second.
  3. x2 + 6x + 9Middle: 3x + 3x = 6x. End: 3 × 3 = 9.
The pattern (x + a)2 = x2 + 2ax + a2 (x − a)2 = x2 − 2ax + a2

An expression like x2 + 6x + 9, which equals a bracket squared, is called a perfect square.

The middle number is the number in front of x. In x2 + 6x + 9 it's 6, which is twice the 3 in the bracket. So:

  • the number in the bracket is half the middle number
  • the last number is that half, squared

That is the whole idea behind completing the square.

Multiply out(x − 5)2
3 · The move

Half it, square it

Now go backwards. Start with x2 + 6x. What number do you add to make a perfect square?

Half of 6 is 3, so the bracket is (x + 3). The number to add is 32 = 9.

  1. Half it. Take half of the middle number. Keep its sign.
  2. Square it. That's the number to add. It is never negative.
  3. Write the bracket. The bracket holds x and the half: (x + half)2.
Make a perfect squarex2 + 6x + ___ = ( ___ )2
  1. half of 6 is 3Half it.
  2. 32 = 9Square it. 9 goes in the blank.
  3. x2 + 6x + 9 = (x + 3)2The bracket holds the half. It's section 2's example, backwards.
Make a perfect squarex2 − 8x + ___ = ( ___ )2
  1. half of −8 is −4Half of a negative number is negative.
  2. (−4)2 = 16Negative times negative is positive: (−4)(−4) = 16.
  3. x2 − 8x + 16 = (x − 4)2The bracket holds the half, minus sign and all. Check the middle term: 2 · (−4) · x = −8x.
Watch the signs
  • The half has the same sign as the middle number.
  • The number you add is always positive.
  • The sign in the bracket matches the middle number: x2 − 8x gives (x − 4)2.

5 · Keep it balanced

In an equation, add it to both sides

An equation is a balance. Whatever you add to the left side, add to the right side too.

Solve by completing the squarex2 − 6x = 7
  1. half of −6 is −3, and (−3)2 = 9This is the number to add.
  2. x2 − 6x + 9 = 7 + 9Add 9 to both sides.
  3. (x − 3)2 = 16The left side is now a perfect square.
  4. x − 3 = ±4Take the square root of both sides. Don't forget the ±.
  5. x = 7  or  x = −1Add 3. Check: 49 − 42 = 7 and 1 + 6 = 7.

6 · Circles, step 1

Read the center and radius off standard form

Every circle problem ends with this form, so learn to read it first.

Standard form (x − h)2 + (y − k)2 = r2 center (h, k),  radius r
Two traps
  • The signs flip. (x + 3)2 means h = −3. (x − 3)2 means h = 3.
  • The right side is r2. If it says 25, the radius is 5.

7 · Circles, step 2

One square to complete

Here only x has a middle term, so there is one square to complete. The number is already on the right.

Find the center and radiusx2 − 4x + y2 = 5
  1. half of −4 is −2, and (−2)2 = 4Only x has a middle term. y2 is already a square.
  2. (x2 − 4x + 4) + y2 = 5 + 4Add 4 to both sides.
  3. (x − 2)2 + y2 = 9Write the group as a square.
  4. center (2, 0),  r = 3y2 = (y − 0)2, so k = 0. And r = √9 = 3.

8 · Circles, step 3

Two squares, from general form

Now the full problem. Move the number, then complete two squares: one for x, one for y.

  1. Group the x's, group the y's, move the number to the right.
  2. For each letter: half the middle number, square it, add it to both sides.
  3. Write each group as a square. Add up the right side.
  4. Read the center from the brackets (flip the signs) and r from the square root.
Find the center and radiusx2 + y2 + 6x − 10y + 18 = 0
  1. x2 + 6x + y2 − 10y = −18Group the letters. The +18 becomes −18 on the right.
  2. (x2 + 6x + 9) + (y2 − 10y + 25) = −18 + 9 + 25Half of 6 is 3, squared is 9. Half of −10 is −5, squared is 25. Both go on the right too.
  3. (x + 3)2 + (y − 5)2 = 16Each bracket is a square. −18 + 34 = 16.
  4. center (−3, 5),  r = 4Flip the signs inside the brackets. r = √16 = 4.

9 · Special cases

Four that look different, but use the same steps

A letter has no middle term
Startx2 + y2 − 10y = 0
StepAdd 25 to both sides.x2 + (y2 − 10y + 25) = 25
Resultx2 + (y − 5)2 = 25
Only y needs completing. x² is already (x − 0)², so the center is (0, 5) and the radius is 5.
A number in front of x² and y²
Start2x2 + 2y2 − 8x + 12y − 6 = 0
StepDivide every term by 2.x2 + y2 − 4x + 6y − 3 = 0
Result(x − 2)2 + (y + 3)2 = 16
Divide first, then complete the square as usual. Center (2, −3), radius 4.
The radius isn't a whole number
Start(x − 1)2 + (y + 2)2 = 20
Stepr = √20 = √4 · √5
Resultr = 2√5
Simplify the root and leave it exact. Center (1, −2).
The middle number is odd
Startx2 + 5x + ___
StepHalf it, then square it.half of 5 is 52,  and (52)2 = 254
Resultx2 + 5x + 254 = (x + 52)2
Same steps, with fractions. Keep them as fractions, not decimals.

When the right side isn't positive

If the right side comes out 0, only the center itself works: it's a single point, not a circle. If it comes out negative, nothing works, because two squares added together can't be negative. Try the last two examples above.

14 · Keep this

Cheat sheet

Circle in general form → center and radius

  1. A number in front of x2 and y2? Divide every term by it.
  2. Group the x's, group the y's, move the number to the right.It changes sign when it moves.
  3. Half the middle number, square it, add to both sides.Once for x, once for y. A letter with no middle term needs nothing.
  4. Write each group as (letter + half)2, keeping the half's sign. Add up the right side.
  5. Center: flip the signs in the brackets. Radius: square root of the right side.Right side 0: a single point. Negative: no graph at all.
