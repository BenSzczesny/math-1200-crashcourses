Dividing one polynomial by another

1 · The answer

Quotient, plus remainder over divisor

Start with numbers. 17 ÷ 5 is 3, remainder 2. So 17 = 5 · 3 + 2, and 175 = 3 + 25.

Polynomials work the same way. You divide the dividend by the divisor. The answer on top is the quotient. What's left at the end is the remainder.

How to write the answer dividenddivisor = quotient + remainderdivisor If the remainder is 0, the divisor goes in evenly, and the answer is just the quotient.

You can always check an answer: divisor × quotient + remainder gives back the dividend.

2 · Long division

Divide, multiply, subtract, bring down

Write the dividend with its powers in order, highest first. Then repeat four moves until there's nothing left to bring down.

  1. Divide the first term that's left by the first term of the divisor. Write the result on top.
  2. Multiply that result by the whole divisor. Write it underneath.
  3. Subtract that line: change both of its signs, then add.
  4. Bring down the next term, and go back to step 1.
Divide(4x2 − 15x + 11) ÷ (x − 2)
  1. set upx − 2 outside,  4x2 − 15x + 11 insideThe divisor goes on the left. The dividend goes under the bar, powers in order. The quotient will be written on top.
  2. divide4x2 ÷ x = 4xDivide the two circled terms: the first term inside by the first term of the divisor. Write 4x on top, over the x column.
  3. multiply4x(x − 2) = 4x2 − 8xThe new term times the whole divisor. Write it underneath in brackets with a minus in front: this whole line gets subtracted.
  4. subtract−15x − (−8x) = −7xThe 4x2 terms cancel, as the first terms always do. In the circled column, the minus turns −8x into +8x.
  5. bring down+11The next term of the dividend comes straight down. Now −7x + 11 is the new line to work on.
  6. divide−7x ÷ x = −7Start again with the new first term. Write −7 on top, over the number column.
  7. multiply−7(x − 2) = −7x + 14Times the whole divisor again.
  8. subtract11 − 14 = −3The −7x terms cancel. Nothing is left to bring down, so −3 is the remainder.
  9. 4x − 7 − 3x − 2Check: (x − 2)(4x − 7) − 3 = 4x2 − 15x + 14 − 3. That's the dividend.
Subtract the whole line

The minus in front of the brackets changes both signs: −(4x2 − 8x) = −4x2 + 8x. Forgetting the second sign is the most common slip.

3 · Divisors like 2x + 5

Divide by the first term only

A divisor like 2x + 5 changes one thing. In the divide step, divide by its first term, 2x. Then multiply by the whole divisor, as before.

Divide(6x2 + 11x − 4) ÷ (2x + 5)
  1. set up2x + 5 outside,  6x2 + 11x − 4 insideSame set-up as before.
  2. divide6x2 ÷ 2x = 3xOnly the circled 2x, not the whole divisor. Divide the numbers, 6 ÷ 2 = 3, then the powers, x2 ÷ x = x.
  3. multiply3x(2x + 5) = 6x2 + 15xNow the whole divisor.
  4. subtract11x − 15x = −4xThe 6x2 terms cancel.
  5. bring down−4The new line is −4x − 4.
  6. divide−4x ÷ 2x = −2Again by the first term of the divisor only.
  7. multiply−2(2x + 5) = −4x − 10Times the whole divisor.
  8. subtract−4 − (−10) = 6Nothing left to bring down. The remainder is 6.
  9. 3x − 2 + 62x + 5The remainder goes over the whole divisor, 2x + 5.

4 · Missing powers

Write 0 for a missing power

2x3 − 5x + 3 has no x2 term. Long division needs a column for every power, so write it in as 0x2. Skip it, and terms land in the wrong columns.

Divide(2x3 − 5x + 3) ÷ (x − 2)
  1. set up2x3 + 0x2 − 5x + 3Fill the gap with 0x2 first, so every power has a column.
  2. divide2x3 ÷ x = 2x2It goes on top, over the x2 column.
  3. multiply2x2(x − 2) = 2x3 − 4x2Times the whole divisor.
  4. subtract0x2 − (−4x2) = 4x2This is where the 0x2 gets used.
  5. bring down−5xThe new line is 4x2 − 5x.
  6. divide4x2 ÷ x = 4xStart the cycle again.
  7. multiply4x(x − 2) = 4x2 − 8xTimes the whole divisor.
  8. subtract−5x − (−8x) = 3xThe minus turns −8x into +8x.
  9. bring down+3The new line is 3x + 3.
  10. divide3x ÷ x = 3One more cycle.
  11. multiply3(x − 2) = 3x − 6Times the whole divisor.
  12. subtract3 − (−6) = 9Nothing left to bring down: 9 is the remainder.
  13. 2x2 + 4x + 3 + 9x − 2The quotient is one power lower than the dividend: x3 ÷ x gives x2.
Divide(x3 − 3x + 4) ÷ (x − 1)
5 · Synthetic division

The shortcut for dividing by x − c

When the divisor is x − c, you can leave out the x's and work with the numbers only. It only works for a divisor like this. For one like 2x + 5, use long division.

The number in the box x − c  →  c x − 2 → 2  ·  x + 3 → −3  ·  the number that makes the divisor 0
Same problem as section 4(2x3 − 5x + 3) ÷ (x − 2)
  1. set upbox 2,  row 2, 0, −5, 32 goes in the box, since x − 2 = 0 at x = 2. Then the coefficients in order, with 0 for the missing x2. Leave a row of space, then a line.
  2. bring down2The first coefficient comes straight down below the line.
  3. multiply2 · 2 = 4The box number times the number you just wrote below the line. Write the 4 in the next column, in the middle row.
  4. add0 + 4 = 4Add down the column: the coefficient plus the number under it. Write the sum below the line.
  5. multiply4 · 2 = 8Box number times the newest number below the line. Into the next column.
  6. add−5 + 8 = 3Add down the column.
  7. multiply3 · 2 = 6Into the last column.
  8. add3 + 6 = 9The last column's sum is the remainder. It gets a box.
  9. 2x2 + 4x + 3 + 9x − 2Read the bottom row. 2, 4, 3 is the quotient, starting one power lower than the dividend. 9 is the remainder. The same answer as long division.
Add, don't subtract

Long division subtracts. Synthetic division adds, because the sign was already flipped when c went in the box.

Use synthetic division.(x3 − 4x2 + 2x + 5) ÷ (x + 1)

Cheat sheet

Dividing polynomials

  1. Powers in order, with 0 for any missing power.2x3 − 5x + 3 → 2x3 + 0x2 − 5x + 3
  2. Long division: divide by the first term of the divisor, multiply by the whole divisor, subtract (change both signs), bring down. Repeat.
  3. Synthetic division, only for x − c: put c in the box. Bring down, multiply, add. Repeat.x + 3 puts −3 in the box.
  4. Read the answer: the quotient is one power lower than the dividend. The last number is the remainder.
  5. Write it as quotient + remainderdivisor.
