1 · The leading term

The term with the highest power of x

Standard form Multiplied out, like terms combined, and written from the highest power of x down to the lowest. for example  4x3 − 2x2 + x − 7

In standard form, the leading term is the first term: the one with the highest power of x.

  • The degree is that power.
  • The leading coefficient is the number in front of it, sign included.

2 · From factored form

Don't multiply it all out

You could multiply it out to standard form, but you don't need to. To find the leading term of a product, multiply the leading term of each factor. The other terms only make smaller powers.

A power on a bracket applies to its leading term too: the leading term of (2x − 5)2 is (2x)2 = 4x2.

Leading term off(x) = −5x(2x − 5)2
  1. −5x  and  (2x)2 = 4x2The leading term of each factor. Square the 2 as well as the x.
  2. −5x · 4x2 = −20x3Degree 3, leading coefficient −20.
Watch for a hidden minus

In (3 − x) the leading term is −x, not 3. The x term leads, minus sign and all.

The leading term of4(x + 1)2(3 − x)

3 · Why it works

Far from 0, the leading term wins

Take f(x) = x3 − 10x2. Near 0 the second term matters. Far out, it doesn't:

xx3−10x2f(x)
1001,000,000−100,000900,000
1,0001,000,000,000−10,000,000990,000,000

The higher power grows much faster, so it takes over. That is why the ends of the graph follow the leading term, and only the leading term.

4 · The rule

Two questions: even or odd? Plus or minus?

Every polynomial's ends look like one of these four.

End behavior Even degree: both ends go the same way. Odd degree: the ends go opposite ways. Positive leading coefficient: rises on the right. Negative: falls on the right.

So read the right end from the sign, then get the left end from even or odd.

End behavior off(x) = 2x3 − x4 + 6
  1. leading term −x4The highest power is 4. It's written second, and the minus belongs to it.
  2. negative: falls on the rightThe sign decides the right end.
  3. 4 is even: the left end matches, so it falls tooEven degree sends both ends the same way.
  4. falls left, falls rightThe same ends as y = −x2.
End behavior off(x) = 5x3 − x + 1
  1. leading term 5x3Degree 3, leading coefficient 5.
  2. positive: rises on the rightThe sign decides the right end.
  3. 3 is odd: the left end is the opposite, so it fallsOdd degree sends the ends opposite ways.
  4. falls left, rises rightThe same ends as y = x3.
End behavior off(x) = −2x5 + x2

It works backwards too. From a graph, the ends tell you even or odd, and plus or minus.
