The ends of a graph

Far to the left and far to the right, a rational function acts like something much simpler: its leading term over its leading term. Simplify that, and a basic graph tells you where the ends go.

This joins two things you've already met: end behavior of polynomials and horizontal asymptotes. They are one idea. If finding a leading term feels shaky, do End Behavior first. 1 · The idea

Far out, only the leading terms matter

A rational function is one polynomial divided by another. Take f(x) = 2x2 + 3xx2 − 5. Watch what happens as x gets big:

xtopbottomf(x)
1023095about 2.42
10020,3009,995about 2.03
1,0002,003,000999,995about 2.003

The values close in on 2. The reason: the highest power grows much faster than everything else. When x is huge, the 3x and the −5 hardly change anything. So the top is about 2x2 and the bottom is about x2, and 2x2x2 = 2.

The same thing happens far to the left. At x = −1,000 the value is about 1.997.

The big idea Far to the left and far to the right, f(x) ≈ leading term of the topleading term of the bottom ≈ means "is close to"

What "the ends" means

Here is the same idea as a picture. The first graph is f, with its middle shaded out because we aren't looking there. The second graph is the whole graph of just the leading terms, which is the line y = 2.

Look only outside the shading. Those two pieces, the far left and the far right, are the ends. There, f stays close to the line y = 2, and it gets closer the farther out you go.

The leading terms tell you nothing about what's under the shading. Near the middle the smaller terms still matter. Part 2 and Part 3 deal with the middle.

A polynomial fits this too. Its bottom is just 1, so its ends follow its leading term. That's end behavior.

2 · Simplify

Leading terms over each other, then cancel

Now simplify that fraction the way you'd simplify any fraction: divide the numbers, subtract the powers of x. There are only three ways it can come out.

From factored form

Don't multiply it out. The leading term of a product is the product of the leading terms, so take the x term from each bracket. A power on a bracket applies to its leading term too.

  2. bottom: x2The bracket is squared, so its x is squared.

  3. −x2x3 = −1xSubtract the powers: 3 − 2 = 1, and the x that's left is on the bottom.

3 · The basic graphs

Know these, and you know the ends

Once it's simplified, you have one of a few basic graphs. Learn their ends. The dashed red lines are asymptotes.

A number in front stretches the graph, but the ends go the same way. A minus flips it over the x-axis. 1x3 has the same ends as 1x, and 1x4 has the same ends as 1x2.

Look along the cards. The two questions from End Behavior work for all of them, even with the power on the bottom:

Two questions Plus or minus? Plus: the right end is above the x-axis. Minus: it's below. Even or odd power of x? Even: the left end is on the same side as the right. Odd: it's on the other side.

8 · Keep this

Cheat sheet

The ends of a graph

  1. Far out, f ≈ leading term over leading term. A polynomial has a bottom of 1.
  2. Factored form: multiply the leading term of each bracket. Watch for a hidden minus.(3 − x) leads with −x
  3. Simplify. Divide the numbers, subtract the powers.
  4. Just a number c: horizontal asymptote y = c.
  5. x on the bottom: horizontal asymptote y = 0.
  6. x on the top: no horizontal asymptote. The ends go up or down.
  7. Which side? Plus: right end above. Minus: below. Even power or a number: left end on the same side. Odd: the other side.
