Graphing rational functions · Part 2 of 3

Even keeps, odd changes

A graph can only change sides of the x-axis at a zero or a vertical asymptote. The power on the factor decides whether it does: an odd power changes the sign, and an even power keeps it.

This joins two things you've met: multiplicity of zeros and vertical asymptotes. One rule covers both. If finding zeros or asymptotes feels shaky, do Zeros and Multiplicity or Asymptotes and Holes first. 1 · The rule

Four pictures, one rule

First factor the top and the bottom all the way, into linear factors. A linear factor has x to the first power and nothing higher, like x − 3 or 2x + 1. A plain x counts too. On this page, "factor" always means a linear factor.

A power outside the bracket isn't part of the factor. (x − 3)2 is the linear factor x − 3 with power 2, and x2 is the linear factor x with power 2. But x2 − 9 isn't finished. It factors into (x − 3)(x + 3).

Each linear factor gives a key number: the value of x that makes it 0. So x − 3 gives 3, and 2x + 1 gives −12. Where the factor sits tells you what the key number is:

  • A linear factor on the top gives a zero, where the graph meets the x-axis.
  • A linear factor on the bottom gives a vertical asymptote, where the graph shoots up or down.

Something like x2 + 1 has no linear factors. It's never 0, so it gives no key number.

The multiplicity is the power on the linear factor, on the top or the bottom. No power written means power 1.

A minus sign flips the graph, so up and down swap. It doesn't change what the power does: odd still changes the sign, and even still keeps it. Top or bottom and the power tell you which card you have. The rest of the function tells you whether it's flipped. Walk Across the Graph shows how to tell.

2 · Why

Watch one factor change sign

Look at (x − 2) just left and just right of 2, with different powers:

Factorat x = 1at x = 3Sign
x − 2−11changes
(x − 2)211stays
(x − 2)3−11changes
(x − 2)411stays

Squaring makes any number positive, so an even power is positive on both sides. An odd power keeps the minus of x − 2, so it's negative on the left and positive on the right.

The other factors don't change sign at 2, because they aren't 0 there. So f changes sign at 2 exactly when this one factor does.

Top or bottom makes no difference. Dividing by a negative flips the sign just as multiplying by one does. That's why one rule covers zeros and asymptotes.

Cancel first

If the same factor is on the top and the bottom, cancel it before you count. A factor that cancels completely leaves a hole. The graph passes straight through with one point missing, so the sign stays. If some of the factor is left, count the power that's left.

7 · Keep this

Cheat sheet

Even keeps, odd changes

  1. Factor into linear factors, then cancel. Linear means x to the first power, like x − 3. A factor that cancels completely is a hole. The sign stays there.
  2. Top factors are zeros. Bottom factors are vertical asymptotes. The power is the multiplicity.
  3. Odd power: the sign changes. A zero crosses. At an asymptote, it's up on one side and down on the other.
  4. Even power: the sign stays. A zero touches. At an asymptote, it's both up or both down.
  5. Why: an even power is positive on both sides. An odd power keeps the factor's sign, which changes there.
